Thermal management has become a first-order constraint in chip and package design, yet heat sink geometries are still dominated by a handful of generic, catalog-style form factors. Tailoring the heat sink to the specific spatial power map of a chip can substantially reduce junction temperature, but the design space is high-dimensional, non-differentiable with respect to full thermal simulation, and, critically, lacks the ground-truth optimal designs that supervised learning would require.

We present DiffCool, a label-free generative framework that synthesizes chip-tailored heat sinks via thermal-aware diffusion. DiffCool learns the distribution of manufacturable heat sink geometries from a dataset of existing designs and steers sampling with a thermal-aware guidance signal derived from the chip thermal map, so that generated structures concentrate material where the heat flux is highest. Because guidance is provided by a differentiable surrogate of the thermal response instead of paired optimal designs, DiffCool requires no labeled training data, which is the key obstacle to applying generative models in this domain.

Experiments on realistic chip power maps under practical heat sink design rules show that DiffCool consistently produces designs with lower peak temperature and thermal resistance than conventional and heuristic baselines, while remaining within manufacturing constraints and offering a controllable trade-off among cooling performance, material usage, and pressure drop.
