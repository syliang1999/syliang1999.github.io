Integrated voltage regulators (IVRs) embedded in advanced packages enable fine-grained power delivery, but their benefits depend strongly on how their placement interacts with the vertical power delivery network (PDN). Optimizing IVR placement in isolation ignores the routing cost and IR drop of the vertical interconnects that feed them, while routing the vertical PDN without regard to IVR placement yields suboptimal power integrity.

We formulate vertical PDN routing and embedded IVR placement as a joint co-optimization problem and propose an efficient solution that alternates between the two tightly coupled subproblems. The placement stage selects IVR locations that minimize the electrical distance to the load while respecting thermal and area constraints, and the routing stage allocates and routes vertical interconnects under advanced-packaging manufacturing rules, with a fast IR-drop and congestion evaluator closing the loop between the two stages.

Experimental results on representative package designs demonstrate that the proposed co-optimization reduces IR drop and vertical routing resources simultaneously compared with decoupled flows, highlighting the importance of jointly considering power delivery and placement in next-generation packaging.
