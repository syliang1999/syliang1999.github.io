Thermo-mechanical simulation has become indispensable for the design and reliability assessment of advanced packages, but its accuracy hinges on the orthotropic thermo-mechanical properties—such as anisotropic elastic moduli, Poisson ratios, and coefficients of thermal expansion—of each constituent material. These properties are notoriously difficult to obtain, since direct measurement requires dedicated specimens and elaborate experiments, while classical inverse approaches based on iterative finite-element analysis are prohibitively slow for the many-layer, many-material stacks of modern packages.

We propose CUTh-Extractor, a vision-transformer (ViT)-based framework for the efficient extraction of orthotropic thermo-mechanical material properties. Rather than solving the inverse problem iteratively, CUTh-Extractor learns a direct mapping from measured response fields—for example, full-field deformation or temperature data—to the underlying material parameters, exploiting the strong global feature-modeling capability of the ViT architecture. A physics-consistent training pipeline built on simulation-generated data enables the model to generalize across materials and stack configurations while remaining robust to measurement noise.

Experiments on representative advanced-packaging cases show that CUTh-Extractor recovers orthotropic material properties with high accuracy and orders-of-magnitude faster runtime than conventional inverse-analysis flows, enabling rapid, low-cost material characterization for package design, simulation, and reliability analysis.
