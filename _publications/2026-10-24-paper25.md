Active-matrix digital microfluidic (AM-DMF) platforms provide high-throughput, individually addressable droplet manipulation for biochemical protocols. However, the protocols executed on such platforms are frequently uncertain: some operations may fail, droplet volumes may vary, and the order or the selection of steps may depend on intermediate outcomes. Existing synthesis flows either assume a fixed, deterministic protocol or handle uncertainty at the cost of long synthesis times and heavily over-provisioned electrodes.

We propose a two-layer decoupled synthesis framework that separates the deterministic assignment of operations to the AM-DMF electrode array from the handling of protocol-level uncertainty. The upper layer synthesizes a robust protocol graph that encodes alternatives and contingency paths for uncertain operations, while the lower layer maps this graph onto the platform with a scheduler that reuses resources across branches and minimizes the number of electrodes required. This decoupling keeps each layer tractable and allows uncertainty to be resolved at runtime without re-synthesizing the entire design.

Experimental results on uncertain biochemical protocol benchmarks demonstrate that the framework reduces electrode usage and completion time compared with uncertainty-oblivious and monolithic approaches, while guaranteeing that all contingent execution paths remain feasible on the generated platform.
