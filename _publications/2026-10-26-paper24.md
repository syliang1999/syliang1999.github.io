Flow-based microfluidic biochips require a control layer that delivers pressure to the valves of the flow layer, and the routing of this control layer largely determines whether a chip can be reliably fabricated and operated. Existing control-layer routers focus on routability and wirelength, leaving the design vulnerable to defects, pressure drop, and valve-actuation failures that only become apparent after fabrication.

This paper presents a reliability-aware control-layer routing method for flow-based microfluidic biochips based on Bayesian optimization. We define a reliability metric that captures the effects of long control channels, shared pressure paths, and vulnerable routing topologies on valve actuation, and search the routing solution space with Bayesian optimization to trade off reliability against conventional constraints such as channel crossing count and routing area. A fast reliability evaluator makes the search tractable for practical chip sizes.

Experimental results on a suite of real and synthetic biochip benchmarks show that the proposed method improves control-layer reliability while maintaining routing legality and competitive wirelength.
